Shoyoroku Case 45

Four Phrases from the Engaku Sutra

This page gathers materials from my study of Case 45 from the Shoyoroku (Book of Equanimity). I am preparing a Hossen talk on this case for July 2025 at the San Luis Obispo Zen Circle.

"At all times, you do not raise the delusive mind.
When there are all kinds of illusory thoughts, you do not extinguish them.
Dwelling in the delusory state of mind, you do not add understanding.
Where there is no understanding, you do not distinguish the truth."

The Engaku Sutra (Sutra of Complete Enlightenment)

This case speaks directly to the heart of both Zen practice and The Work. The instruction to neither raise delusive thoughts nor try to extinguish them mirrors Katie's invitation to meet our thoughts with understanding rather than war. "Loving what is" and "not adding understanding where there is no understanding" point to the same freedom.

January 2025

Beginning the Study

Starting to gather materials and deepen my understanding of Case 45. The four phrases from the Engaku Sutra present a radical teaching: we neither grasp at enlightenment nor push away delusion. This is not passivity but complete presence.

Yamada Koun's commentary illuminates how "genjō-kōan" (manifestation of the essence) points to each moment as the complete expression of Buddha nature. There is nothing to add, nothing to remove.
